Fair credit for the vendors who open the market at 4 AM

The cost of urgency

Informal lenders charge about 10% a day. Over a year that is far above the 300% our first estimate showed. Even a simple, non-compounding yearly equivalent is 3,650%.

The access barrier

Banks ask for income proofs, GST registration and home documents. Most stall owners cannot produce them, so the local lender is the only option that is open at 4 AM.

The gap

Vendors need instant working capital before the market day begins, and a way to repay that follows the rhythm of their sales.

How Sammunnati works

  1. Consent

    The vendor chooses which data to share: UPI transaction summaries and past repayments. Sharing can be switched off at any time.

  2. Alternative score

    A transparent score from selling days, steadiness of sales, repayment record and UPI history. No paperwork.

  3. Right-sized advance

    An early-morning advance sized to the vendor's average daily sales, with the full cost shown before accepting.

  4. Micro-sweep repayment

    A fixed share of each UPI payment goes toward the advance until it is repaid. A slow day means a smaller repayment, not a missed one.

Built to avoid the risks we named

Our own technical review listed four risks: consent, responsible limits, repayment shocks and trust in a new lender. Each one has a design answer.

  • Consent first. Each data source is a separate choice with a purpose and an end date. Only UPI summaries and repayment history are required.
  • Responsible limits. The advance is capped at a fraction of average daily sales and stepped down after weak repayment, so no vendor is lent more than a day can repay.
  • No repayment shocks. Repayment is a share of each sale, not a fixed sum due at sunset. Any balance carries to the next market day with no penalty charge.
  • Trust. The full cost is shown before the vendor accepts. Screens are available in Telugu, Hindi and English, and every score comes with plain-language reasons.
  • Regulated lending. In the proposed model the money comes from an RBI-regulated bank or NBFC partner. Sammunnati provides consent, scoring and collection tooling. No partner has been signed yet.
  • Honest limits of the data. UPI only shows part of a stall's sales. Cash-heavy vendors start with smaller limits, and can share an optional cash log to improve their score.

How the system fits together

Money moves out in the morning and back during the day, and every repayment feeds the next score.

Proposed production stack

Installable mobile web app, Node or Python API, PostgreSQL, a consent manager integration, and SMS or WhatsApp messages in the vendor's language.

Scoring approach

Version one is a rule-based score that anyone can read, as in the demo. A statistical model can replace it once the pilot produces repayment data.

Questions people ask

Is Sammunnati a lender?

Not in the proposed model. A regulated bank or NBFC partner lends the money. Sammunnati handles consent, scoring and collections. The partner has not been signed yet.

What if I sell mostly in cash?

The score uses UPI sales, so cash-heavy stalls start with a smaller limit. You can share an optional cash log to give a fuller picture.

What if sales are slow one day?

Repayment is a share of each sale, so a slow day means a smaller payment. Anything left carries to the next market day with no penalty charge, and the next limit is adjusted.

Can I take back my consent?

Yes. You can stop data sharing at any time. An advance already taken stays repayable, but no new data is read.

Is the 0.10% a day fee final?

No. It is an assumption for the prototype. The pilot will test it against the partner's cost of funds and real repayment behaviour.

Does this replace government schemes?

No. Schemes such as PM SVANidhi serve longer-term loans. Sammunnati targets the daily gap between paying for stock at 4 AM and getting paid through the day.
